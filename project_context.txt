PROJECT HANDOFF: MONOCULAR DEPTH-LOCKED VISUAL ACUITY
=====================================================

PURPOSE
-------
This file is the current implementation handoff for the repository. It
describes the running application, especially the Validation page's real-data
workflow and the proposed PO1-PO13 pilot analysis. Source code is authoritative
if it differs from this document.


1. PROJECT SUMMARY
------------------
This browser-based research prototype estimates camera-to-eye distance from
webcam face/iris landmarks using an IPD-based pinhole model. The estimated
distance drives the physical size of a vector Tumbling E so its visual angle
tracks the selected logMAR level.

The project is an engineering research prototype. It is not a clinical
diagnostic device or a clinically validated visual-acuity assessment. Camera
calibration and a measured physical screen calibration are important to the
measurement workflow.


2. CURRENT VALIDATION PAGE
--------------------------
The Validation page (`src/pages/Validation.tsx`) contains:
- A physical-reference distance input, measured independently from the webcam
  estimate.
- A three-second capture of valid raw and EMA distance samples from the live
  production tracking pipeline.
- Recorded-only distance error summaries and CSV/JSON exports.
- A recorded EMA analysis section with raw-versus-EMA graph, sample statistics,
  participant summaries, pilot-derived EMA thresholds, and the result for a
  newly completed three-second recording.

There is no simulated sample generation in the Validation page. Older simulator
and algorithm-analysis modules may remain elsewhere in the repository, but they
are not mounted in this page and must never be presented as participant data.
No real recording should be fabricated or generated to fill a missing cohort
row.

Participant identity and table separation:
- The cohort labels are PO1 through PO13.
- Existing legacy identifiers P01 through P13 (and unpadded PO/P forms) are
  normalized to PO1 through PO13 for analysis.
- The separate PO1-PO13 table always lists all 13 identifiers. A participant
  with no eligible real recording is shown as awaiting data and contributes no
  values to cohort summaries.
- "Recorded distance results — additional recordings" contains only eligible
  real recordings with no participant ID. Its summary statistics and downloads
  use the same subset. Any participant-linked recordings must not appear in
  that table or those exports; records with unsupported participant IDs are
  excluded from both this table and the PO1-PO13 cohort summary.
- The EMA session graph can inspect any eligible saved real recording, whether
  or not it has a PO1-PO13 association.
- Only records tagged `RECORDED` enter recorded-human analysis. Records with
  missing or other source tags are excluded; the Validation page does not show
  a separate source-review section. Invalid `RECORDED` attempts are listed
  with their status/reason and are not used in metrics.
- The cohort count is derived from saved, eligible records. Do not claim 13
  participants have complete usable data until the saved records have been
  checked for identity, validity, and protocol completeness.
- The new-user result uses only the just-saved, unlinked recording from the
  current three-second capture. It is not added to the PO1-PO13 pilot cohort.
- Pilot thresholds and categories are unavailable until at least two pilot
  participants have eligible recordings. A valid new-user EMA SD can still be
  displayed without assigning a category while thresholds are unavailable.

The page stores numerical observations in the browser's IndexedDB validation
store. It does not store webcam images or video. Existing numerical records
must not be deleted as part of routine UI changes.


3. RESEARCH QUESTION AND PROPOSED PILOT ANALYSIS
------------------------------------------------
The proposed analysis is whether EMA smoothing reduces short-term variability
in webcam distance tracking while preserving agreement with a physical
reference. The intended pilot cohort comprises 13 real participants
(PO1-PO13), each contributing eligible three-second recordings. The participant
is the unit for cohort summaries: first average eligible recordings within a
participant, then calculate the cohort distribution so participants with more
recordings do not dominate.

For every eligible recording, report:
- Number of valid temporal samples and capture duration.
- Raw and EMA mean distance.
- Raw and EMA sample standard deviation (SD).
- Raw and EMA absolute distance error relative to the physical reference.
- EMA noise reduction.

For the PO1-PO13 cohort, calculate each participant's EMA sample SD for every
eligible recording, then average those recording-level SDs within that
participant. Calculate Q1, median, and Q3 from the resulting participant-level
values, so each participant contributes at most one value. Use the existing
linear-interpolation quantile method. Thresholds remain unavailable until at
least two participants have eligible values.

Classification boundaries:
- Low: participant/new-user EMA SD < Q1.
- Medium: EMA SD >= Q1 and <= Q3, including equality at either boundary.
- High: EMA SD > Q3.

The Validation page classifies the just-saved valid three-second unlinked
recording as a new user. It uses the same sample SD method and centimetre
units as the pilot. These cohort-relative categories describe variability
compared with the observed pilot only; they do not diagnose eyesight, explain
the cause of variability, or establish accuracy. These quartiles describe
this pilot; they are not preselected limits, proof of clinical acceptability,
or universally applicable thresholds. Lower SD means lower observed
variability, not necessarily greater accuracy. Interpret stability together
with absolute error and assess any candidate threshold in an independent,
larger cohort.

Display the limitation note: "These thresholds are exploratory and derived
from a small pilot cohort. They are not clinically validated or universal
performance limits."

Do not state numerical participant results or quartiles in a paper until the
actual saved/exported recordings have been checked. A nominal cohort size of
13 is not proof that all 13 participants have complete valid recordings.

Suggested IEEE proposal wording:

"We propose to characterize short-term distance-tracking variability using
the three-second webcam recordings from a 13-participant pilot cohort
(PO1-PO13), with independently measured physical distance as the reference.
For each participant, EMA sample standard deviations will be averaged across
eligible recordings and used to derive exploratory cohort Q1 and Q3
thresholds. A new user's result will be categorized relative to those
thresholds, while distance error and EMA noise reduction are reported
separately. These bands are exploratory descriptions rather than prevalidated
acceptance limits and require confirmation in an independent, larger cohort."

Suggested interpretation of observed bands:
- Below Q1: lower variability than most observed participant values in this
  pilot.
- Q1 through Q3: central half of the observed participant distribution.
- Above Q3: higher variability than most observed participant values in this
  pilot.
- For noise reduction, larger positive values indicate greater reduction of
  temporal SD; a negative value indicates that EMA SD exceeded raw SD.

These statements are descriptive and should not be phrased as pass/fail rules
unless a future study prespecifies and validates such a rule.


4. METRICS AND DATA HANDLING
----------------------------
Temporal samples contain elapsed time, raw distance, EMA-smoothed distance,
and physical reference distance. Invalid or non-positive distance samples are
excluded from the EMA calculations. At least two valid samples are required.

Sample standard deviation uses Bessel's correction (N-1):
  s = sqrt(sum((x_i - x_bar)^2) / (N - 1))

EMA noise reduction is not clamped:
  noise_reduction_percent = ((raw_SD - EMA_SD) / raw_SD) * 100

If raw SD is zero, noise reduction is not applicable. A negative result must
remain negative and indicates noise inflation; it must not be silently changed
to zero.

For each recording:
  absolute_error = abs(mean_EMA_distance - physical_reference_distance)
  percentage_error = absolute_error / physical_reference_distance * 100

The distance results summary reports recording-level MAE, RMSE, maximum error,
and the count within the configured +/-3 cm engineering target. This target is
a prototype benchmark, not a guaranteed performance level or clinical limit.
The additional-record table, its summary, and exports include only rows without
a participant ID. Unsupported participant IDs and unknown provenance are
excluded from these calculations and are not categorized as additional
recordings. Invalid recorded attempts are excluded and identified separately.
The
participant EMA table and pilot quartiles use only valid, real recordings
associated with PO1-PO13. The new-user category is based on the latest newly
saved eligible unlinked recording only and is not merged into the pilot
participant values.

The participant table's missing values are display placeholders only. They are
not zero-valued measurements and must not be included in descriptive
statistics.


5. SIX-REVERSAL SETTING
-----------------------
The production staircase is a 1-up/1-down logMAR procedure. A reversal is a
change in response direction, and the configured stopping rule is six
reversals, subject to a 40-trial maximum.

Defensible proposal language:
"Six reversals were selected as a pragmatic prototype stopping setting to
observe repeated changes in response direction while limiting participant
burden; this setting is not claimed to be optimal or clinically validated."

Important implementation distinction: the current displayed acuity score is
the mean logMAR of the last up to six sequential trials. It does not average
the reversal-point values. A paper must not describe these two scoring methods
as equivalent. Evidence that six reversals is preferable would require a
prespecified comparison against alternatives and validation data; the current
setting alone is not that evidence.


6. APPLICATION ARCHITECTURE
----------------------------
- React and TypeScript single-page application, bundled with Vite.
- `src/context/VisionContext.tsx` owns webcam, landmark tracking, and current
  raw/EMA distance state.
- MediaPipe Face Landmarker runs in the browser. No webcam video is uploaded.
- `src/vision/distanceEstimator.ts` implements the geometric IPD/pinhole
  distance estimate.
- `src/vision/distanceSmoothing.ts` implements the production distance
  smoothing pipeline.
- `src/visionAcuity/` contains optotype sizing, the staircase, and scoring.
- `src/utils/storage.ts` persists trial and validation numerical records in
  IndexedDB.
- `src/utils/csvExport.ts` produces CSV and JSON downloads with provenance
  metadata.
- `src/pages/Validation.tsx` handles the physical reference capture and the
  additional-record distance summary.
- `src/components/RecordedEmaAnalysisSection.tsx` handles recorded EMA
  plotting, the PO1-PO13 table, and exploratory participant-level quartiles.
- `src/components/RealParticipantValidationSection.tsx` is an older
  participant-recording workflow component and is not currently mounted by
  the Validation page. Do not assume it is the active capture route.
- Legacy simulator/algorithm-analysis source files and their tests may remain
  in the repository. They are separate from real recordings and are not
  displayed as Validation results.


7. RELEVANT CONFIGURATION
-------------------------
`src/config.ts`:
- `defaultSmoothingAlpha`: default EMA weight (currently 0.20).
- `targetDistanceToleranceM`: configured distance-error benchmark (currently
  0.03 m).
- `requiredReversals`: staircase reversal stopping value (currently 6).
- `maxTrials`: staircase hard cap (currently 40).

The Validation page samples for three seconds. The real participant recording
helper defines a default protocol of reference distances [1, 1.5, 2] m and
three repetitions per distance, but that helper is not mounted as the current
Validation capture UI. Do not infer completion of that protocol from a
participant label alone.


8. PRIVACY, PROVENANCE, AND SCIENTIFIC INTEGRITY
------------------------------------------------
- Keep video and image processing local to the browser.
- Store only numerical records; never persist or upload camera frames or facial
  images.
- Preserve `dataSource: "RECORDED"` and recording validity information.
- Keep simulations and other generated examples out of PO1-PO13 statistics,
  additional-record summaries, and exports.
- Do not remove a user's saved validation records unless explicitly requested.
- Do not claim that 13 complete records or any specific quartile has been
  verified without checking the actual dataset.
- Distinguish an exploratory pilot description from a validated threshold.
- Do not describe experimental acuity results as clinical diagnoses.

table or its downloads, unknown provenance is identified for review, and
missing participant rows remain absent from calculations.


10. IMPLEMENTATION LIMITATIONS
------------------------------
- The pinhole estimate depends on individual IPD, camera geometry, lighting,
  head position, and calibration quality.
- EMA variability reduction alone does not establish distance accuracy.
- A three-second recording and a 13-person pilot are exploratory and do not
  establish broad population thresholds or clinical validity.
- Quartiles from a small cohort are sensitive to the observed sample and
  quantile method.
- Six reversals are a prototype protocol choice, not a demonstrated optimum.
- The last-six-trials score differs from a reversal-point mean.
- Independent, larger-sample evaluation is needed before promoting any
  proposed stability band or threshold as an acceptance criterion.


11. GUIDANCE FOR FUTURE CHANGES
-------------------------------
1. Preserve clear separation between PO1-PO13 cohort data and additional
   recorded distance results.
2. Keep participant aliases normalized consistently; never silently discard
   valid legacy identifiers.
3. Calculate cohort quartiles from one participant-level value per participant,
   not from a pooled set of frames.
4. Keep negative noise-reduction values and label zero raw SD as not
   applicable.
5. Do not create fabricated measurements to fill missing cohort rows.
6. Maintain physical reference distance as ground truth; do not replace it with

7. Run focused tests, `npm run build`, and the relevant linter after changing
